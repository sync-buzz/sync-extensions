# Routines

A routine is a standing instruction carried out on a clock, whether or not
anybody has a window open. One routine is one record of kind
`routines.routine`, and its body is not a description of the work — it **is**
the work: the text is handed to an agent, on its own, every time the clock
comes round.

That is the whole of what makes writing one different from writing anything
else here. The reader is an agent that has never seen this project, cannot ask
a follow-up, and will do exactly what the text says at three in the morning.

## Writing the body

Write it as an instruction to somebody competent who has just arrived:

- **What to look at**, named. A path, a search, a listing — not "the recent
  work".
- **What counts as worth reporting.** The threshold, stated. Without one, every
  run reports something, because something always changed.
- **What to do about it.** Write a record, say it out loud, open a draft — one
  of those, named.
- **What to do when there is nothing.** Say it explicitly, and the answer is
  almost always *nothing, and say nothing*. A routine that reports "no changes"
  every hour teaches whoever reads it to stop reading.

One thing per routine. A record asking for four unrelated chores is four
routines that fail together and cannot be switched off separately.

## What a routine must not do

It runs with nobody watching, which removes the one safeguard everything else
here relies on.

- **Nothing leaves the machine on a routine's say-so.** Publishing, mailing,
  filing anything anybody outside this project will see: those wait for a
  person, and a routine that arranges one is a routine that got somebody's
  agreement it never asked for.
- **Nothing irreversible.** Deleting records, moving files, rewriting history.
  Reporting that something ought to be deleted is the routine's job; deleting
  it is not.
- **Do not raise one for yourself.** A routine spends tokens on a clock for as
  long as it exists, and the person paying for that decides it. Propose one,
  say what it would cost per day, and wait.

## Switching one on

`enabled` starts false and staying false is the normal state of a routine
somebody is still writing. Turning it on is the moment the cost starts, so it
is somebody's decision rather than the last step of writing the record.

`every` is a closed set because each value is a clock Sync declares; a value
outside it names no clock and the routine simply never runs. Choose the
slowest one that still catches what you are watching for — a fifteen-minute
routine costs ninety-six times a daily one and is usually watching something
that changes weekly.

`agent` names which agent on this machine carries it out. Leave it out unless
the work needs a particular one, and say in the body why if you set it.

## What it writes back

A routine that found something writes it into the project's memory as a record
of whatever kind holds that sort of claim, and links it to nothing unless there
is something to link to. It does not write its findings into its own body: the
body is the instruction, and an instruction that grows a log at the bottom is
an instruction that gets longer every hour.
